Certifying Performance Budgets for Adaptive Byzantine Consensus

Nov 23, 2026; Vera Plischke

Full paper (PDF); OTS Stamp

Abstract

Byzantine consensus protocols may modify their leader and communication channels, but inaccuracies in their measurements can make such modifications expensive, even when agreement holds. We separate the choice of a new configuration from the decision to enact it. The controller aggregates signed evidence under a fixed unknown fault set and reserves the cost of the switch. If the switch preserves the execution prefix, the reserve covers the remaining clipped delay and wire service. This yields a cumulative bound against ordinary fallback recovery that does not require accurate predictions. For a single precharged schedule with protected prefixes, we also bound the cost of providing manipulated (vs. truthful) reports to the same selector, including lost improvements. Signed simulations showed that delayed (vs. eager) copies of votes saved 42.13 ms in selected loss scenarios under the same allowance. A prepare certificate provides a different form of evidence. When it has not reached enough replicas, a replica can still cast its old precommit before voting in the next round. We prove agreement in the presence of ordinary and late signing. With simulated prepare omissions, repair saved 199.70 ms under the same allowance relative to merely relaying a prepare. Silent faults or persistent suppression could slow initial adaptation in the first round.
